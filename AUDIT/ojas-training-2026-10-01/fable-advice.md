## Q0. The framing decision the lead must carry to the human first

**Decision.** "Replace PyTorch with ojas" for the Lappi fine-tune is, concretely: **canonical tessl's Qwen3.5 training kernels on Metal + a Rust trainer, on the M5 Pro, climbing a parity ladder whose rows are all `quick`**, while the GH200 campaign stays on PyTorch. ojas today cannot express Qwen3.5 and has no CUDA backend; tessl already has the whole Qwen3.5-2B training step.

**Rationale.** ojas's `Backend` trait is nanolab-shaped: MHA with `Tq = Tk`, half-split RoPE over the full head, nanolab RMSNorm (`w`, not Qwen's `1 + w`), a per-head sigmoid gate (`[T,H]`, not Qwen's elementwise output gate), cross-entropy that materializes `[rows × vocab]`, f32 only, `METAL_MAX_HEAD_DIM = 128`, no GDN, no conv1d, no checkpointing, no CUDA `Backend` [V `ojas-core/src/backend.rs:60-66,125-135,193-240`; `docs/op-coverage.md` op matrix; `docs/pytorch-parity-plan.md` §1 rows 4, 6, 12, 13, 19]. Qwen3.5-2B needs head_dim **256** with GQA 8:2, GDN 16×128 / 16×128 with conv 4, partial RoPE 0.25, interleaved MRoPE, an attention output gate, gated RMSNorm, a tied 248,320-row head [V `crates/qd-export/tests/fixtures/qwen35_2b_base_config.json`]. Canonical tessl has every one of those forward **and backward**, deterministic, with the chunked-state GDN backward, FA2-style attention backward at D=256/GQA with LSE, a chunked CE over supervised rows that never forms `[N,V]`, in-place AdamW with per-entry weight decay, a bank that sums several sequences' gradients, `Supervise::Rows`, and `PendingStep::hidden` + `train_backward_into(dh)` for a loss outside tessl (the span head) [V `tessl/src/qwen35_train.rs:1-34,247-262,306-330,697-716`; `gdn_train.rs:1-30`; `attn_train.rs:1-20,34`; `cross_entropy.rs:1-37`; `qwen35_adamw.rs:1-20,361-385`]. It is checked against transformers' own autograd on a committed tiny fixture (loss 4.3e-8 rel, 27 gradients within 3.1e-6 of each parameter's max) and, in an `#[ignore]` test, against the real 2B at 128 tokens [R `tessl/docs/qwen35.md:824-880`; V the test bodies at `tests/qwen35_train.rs:199-216,845-882`]. The ojas plan itself lists GDN as "Missing in ojas. Exists Metal-only in tessl" [V `docs/pytorch-parity-plan.md` §1 row 19] and names its framework layer as "proposal, not implemented … awaiting a go/no-go" [V `docs/framework-design.md` Status]. Rule 6 makes tessl the kernel owner anyway. So the question is not "build Qwen3.5 in ojas" but "what does ojas own above tessl" — see Q5's placement decision and human ask 2.

**One tension, said out loud.** `framework-design.md` §3 proposes `Trainer<B>` over `Backend` + `Tape`; tessl's step is a hand-written backward. A Qwen trainer over tessl's step placed in ojas is a second trainer beside the proposed one. The convergence is a minimal step-provider trait (forward → hidden rows → external gradient → backward into a bank; grad-norm; AdamW with per-entry lr/wd) that tessl's `Qwen35Model` implements now and ojas's future Tape model implements later. Whether that trait lands in ojas this session or the trainer starts in Lappi is the human's call (ask 2); this session should not edit ojas regardless (ask 3).

## Q2. Where it runs

**Decision: Mac Metal first (tessl). GH200 via wgpu/Vulkan: refused. CUDA `Backend`: deferred, human decision, weeks.**

**Rationale.** The GH200 has no Metal and ojas has no CUDA `Backend` [V `backends.md` status table]; wgpu has none of the Qwen-specific kernels, no bf16, D≤128, and the box's Vulkan ICD is unverified (no mention in any HANDOFF/AUDIT; `rg` empty) — moot either way. The Mac path exists today. Memory on the M5 Pro (64 GB [V `system_profiler`]): the 2B has ~2.0 B parameters [I from tessl's "16 GB more" for two f32 moments, `qwen35_adamw.rs:5-6`]; f32 weights 8 GB + f32 gradients 8 GB + AdamW moments 16 GB = 32 GB persistent; `train_step` at T=2048 measured 25.8 GB peak footprint including weights and gradients [V `bench/results/qwen35_train_step_bf16_m5pro.txt`], so ≈42 GB at T=2048 with moments [I]. At 8K tokens it is **unmeasured** [U], and smaller than a torch batch suggests: tessl processes **one sequence at a time** into the bank [V `qwen35_train.rs:473-487`], so the per-step scratch is one 8K row's (saved residual stream 24 × 8192 × 2048 × 4 B = 1.6 GB, GDN checkpoints 128 MiB, bounded CE chunks, attention workspace), not four concurrent rows; ~45–50 GB total is plausible [I] against a Metal working-set ceiling tessl probes at `runtime.rs:657` but whose value here I did not read [U]. **The first Mac command measures it with tessl's existing bench at `--step=8192`** (Q5), and the lane adds AdamW's +16 GB on top, since the bench runs `train_step` only. Speed: 345 tok/s on battery, ~2× plugged [V/R same file]; J1's epoch is 74.7 M padded positions in 4,040 s on the GH200 [V `HANDOFF/gh200-phase4-2026-10-01.md` J1] → **30–60 h per seed on the Mac** [I; unpadded tokens are fewer]. So the Mac is the parity-ladder and reduced-schedule machine, not the 3-seed campaign engine; a 3-seed Mac campaign is a separate budget decision after rung (d).
