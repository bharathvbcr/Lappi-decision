## Decision

Rung (d) fits the approved window without exceeding it. It is **200 optimizer steps of F's recipe, unchanged, on the first 200 batches of F's own batch order**, not "10% of the schedule".

1. **Schedule fraction: 200 of F's 9,683 steps (2.07%), fixed before step 0, never raised on the day.** F seed 0 is 9,683 steps [V `HANDOFF/gh200-phase4-2026-10-01.md:501`]. At the measured floor of 452 tok/s and v4's 31,697 unpadded tokens per step (306,926,895 / 9,683; row d96409bd `shard_sequences.detail`), one step is 70.1 s and 200 steps are 14,025 s = **3.9 h**. The real trainer should be faster: the bench's `train_step` runs cross-entropy over every one of the T−1 rows [V `tessl/src/bin/bench_qwen35_train.rs:249-258`] (≈4.8 s of the 18.1 s at T=8192 [I from the per-op table: 1,189 ms at T=2048, linear in T]), while the recipe supervises 0.16% of positions (J4 s0: 152,182 of 93,068,235 [V row 80b19a41 `train.supervised_tokens`]) — ≈52 s/step, ≈2.9 h [I]. The approved 6 h is the hard cap (21,600 s, auto-terminate), which covers 308 steps at the floor.
2. **Subset: a fixed prefix, no selection rule.** The epoch arm's plan is `reader.batches(batch_tokens=35403, seed=config.seed, epoch=0)` [V `tools/real_ft_run.py:9028`], and `config.seed` is the protocol seed **20260919**, not the run seed [V row d96409bd `protocol.seed`; the run seed 0/1/2 governs the span head's init via `torch.manual_seed(seed)` at `backbone.py:983`]. So "F's seed-0 batch order" is the literal triple **(seed=20260919, epoch=0, batch_tokens=35403)**, identical for all three F seeds; rung (d) takes **plan[:200]**. `_plan` shuffles each bucket's members and then shuffles the batches across buckets ("Interleave the buckets. Without this the epoch runs every short sequence before every long one") [V `python/qd_train/shards.py:2383-2400`], so the prefix is already a bucket-stratified random sample. Not "≤ N tokens": per-token cost is nearly flat in T (plugged T=2048 ≈510 tok/s [I from the two op tables], T=8192 452 tok/s) and dropping the composed long rows drops what v4 added. Not a smaller `batch_tokens`: 4× the steps per hour but no longer F's recipe. Not a stratified re-draw: it is one, by construction, with a rule nobody chose.
3. **Warmup and cosine endpoint: the recipe's own formula applied to the truncated count.** `LRSchedule(peak_lr=1e-5, total_steps=200, warmup_steps=max(1, 200//20)=10, min_lr=1e-6)` [V `real_ft_run.py:1823-1824`; `run_control.py:353-380` for `lr_at`]. The last rate used is `lr_at(199)`; the schedule reaches its endpoint. Keeping F's 484-step warmup would make the whole run warmup and the recipe's "peak_lr" a rate it never uses, which `LRSchedule.__post_init__` refuses for exactly that reason [V `run_control.py:347-351`]. The Rust port's `lr_at(0..199)` must be f64 bit-equal to Python's 200 values (L-trainer's test).
4. **Recipe values, read from rows, not retyped.** J4 s0 `80b19a41`: `optimizer_recipe="master"`, `lr=1e-5`, `span_weight=1.0`, `no_memorise=true`, `gradient_checkpointing=true`, `attn_implementation="sdpa"`, `deterministic=false` [V]; F adds `--batch-tokens 35403 --lower-layers-n 8 --lower-layers-lr-scale 0.1` [V `campaign/f-v4-preregistered.json` `recipe.flags`]. `beta2` default 0.999 [V `optim.py:63`]. The master optimizer is `MasterWeightAdamW(groups, lr, betas, fused)` with **eps 1e-8 and weight_decay 0.01 on every parameter of every group** — an uncapped `rg weight_decay` over `python/qd_train/` and `tools/real_ft_run.py` finds only `optim.py:338,403` (and the unrelated `byte_train.py`), and `layerwise_param_groups` writes only `lr_scale` into its groups [V `optim.py:250-283`]. This closes row 10's open question from my advice: the Rust trainer's wd vector is a uniform 0.01 (norms, `A_log`, `dt_bias`, abstain vectors included), **not** tessl's `default_weight_decay`. F does not carry `--fused-adamw` (opt-in [V `real_ft_run.py:8286`]; absent from J4's recipe keys and F's flags; the fused run is the separate tier-B item), so the arms run the non-fused inner AdamW, as F does. The bench's `train_step` excludes the AdamW update [V `bench_qwen35_train.rs:38,673`]; that update moves ≈56 GB per step (weights r/w, grads r, moments r/w) — under 0.5 s [I], so 70.1 s/step stays the floor.
5. **Span-head init is a fixture, not a coincidence.** The head is two bias-free `nn.Linear(H,H)` plus two zero vectors [V `heads.py:309-314`], initialised from the global stream after `torch.manual_seed(seed)` [V `backbone.py:978-999`]. The combined per-step loss includes the span channel from step 0, so the 0.02-nat bound below is unsatisfiable unless all arms start from the same head. L-oracle writes `span_head_init-seed0.safetensors` from `QwenDecisionStep(seed=0)`; the Rust trainer loads it; every arm records its digest. The 2B config's only dropout key is `attention_dropout: 0.0` [V `qwen35_2b_base_config.json:9`], so no other RNG consumer sits in the step [I — not every consumer traced; `--deterministic` on the arms covers the kernels].
6. **The quantity compared, and where torch's 200 points live.** `LossPoint.loss` is the `AccumulationGroup`'s mean over its micro-batches — with `grad_accum=1`, the batch's own value [V `trainer.py:845-863`]: `accumulate` returns the fused, masked letter CE (mean over the supervised rows) and logs span 0.0 on a letter-only batch; `accumulate_span` returns `letter + span_weight × span_loss`, or `span_weight × span_loss` when the batch has no letter rows [V `backbone.py:1143-1185`]. The ft row carries only `train.loss_log_digest`; the points are in the final checkpoint body (`loss_log`, `loss_digest`, `consumed_digest` [V `run_control.py:1480-1484`]) written by `on_checkpoint.final(result.checkpoint)` [V `real_ft_run.py:2477`]. The Rust trainer logs exactly that quantity as the same JSON (`step`, `epoch`, `index`, `loss_hex`) so the two digests are computed by one formula and the comparison verifies each log against its row before reading a number.
7. **Val scoring does not run on the Mac; it runs on the GH200 after post-F item 10, for all three artifacts, same scorer, same device.** The v4 val is 18,223 sequences (16,124 rows) [V row d96409bd `val_shard_slots_written`], the same count J4's eval rows scored (choice 10,985 + span 7,238 [V `campaign/f-j7prime-preregistered.json` avg_np_reading]). On the GH200 that is 719–858 s per checkpoint, $0.46–0.55 [V rows 2f5fe57a/f3612f73/02cf5ff4]. The Mac's only timed scoring of a checkpoint took 1,363 s against the GH200's 75 s on the same set [V rows 20cf3845 vs 6d170b3c, data be74a513] — 18×, so ≈4 h for the v4 val [I], on torch-MPS bf16, which the Mac lane itself ruled out for scoring [V `HANDOFF/mac-gpu-lane-2026-09-29.md:130`]. This is the one deviation from ask 7's wording ("the PyTorch scorer on the Mac"): the scorer change (additive, approved) is device-agnostic; the artifact crosses to the box, which is a lead/human action. GH200 total for rung (d): two torch arms (200 steps at F s0's 1.79 s/step ≈ 6 min; `--deterministic` and fp32 each up to 2–3× that [I]) + 3 scorings + 3 needle length controls (43–160 s each [V]) ≈ 1.5–2 h ≈ $3.5–4.5 at $2.29/h — under rule 4's $20, covered by the human's ask-5 answer.
8. **Mac window contents (≈4 h nominal, 6 h cap):** load the f32 snapshot and the head fixture → 200 steps → export (f32→bf16 RNE tower + f32 head + manifest via qd-export) **and the f32 masters as a second artifact** (tessl `write_parameters`, ≈8 GB; criterion 4 needs them) → the `ft` row with `quick=True` → an export smoke only (L-scorer's load-and-decode on ≥24 val prompts, the harness rung (c)(ii) uses; finite, manifest digests verified; **no val number is claimed from the Mac**). The trainer process opens `shards/train` only, through the rule-3 door; val is opened by the smoke and by the GH200 scorer, never by a training process; held-out never.

## Preconditions (each recorded in the row's `env`; any one failing means the run does not start)

- **Memory ceiling read before the window, on the CPU side.** tessl's budget is `0.9 × device.recommendedMaxWorkingSetSize()` [V `tessl/src/runtime.rs:657-659`]; on 64 GB that is typically ≈43 GB [I] against the 30.78 GB step footprint + 16 GB moments ≈ 47 GB [I; the bench's step includes the f32 weights and the gradient bank, so the 16 GB is the only addition]. If 47 GB exceeds the budget, the design as written does not start; the contingency is AdamW's moments in host memory with the update on the CPU (new Rust, tested against torch; ≈+5–10 s/step [I], ≈+30 min over 200 steps), costed by the lead before the window — not bf16 moments, which would be a different optimizer from F's.
- **Plugged in, low-power mode off** (`pmset -g batt` shows AC; `pmset -g | grep powermode` is not 1). The T=2048 bench on battery in low-power mode was 2× slower [V bench header]. The 8K bench was on AC: `pmset -g log` shows "Using AC(Charge: 85)" at 15:06 and 15:09 CDT with no later source change, and the log's mtime is 17:37 CDT [V]; its powermode at the time is unrecorded [U] (now 2). So 452 tok/s is a plugged-in number and a floor only under these conditions.
- **GPU exclusive**, agreed with the ojas coordinator for the window (the Mac GPU queue in the HANDOFF).
- Rungs (a), (b), (c) green rows exist; L-tessl's per-entry `lr_scale` landed; the schedule bit-equality test and the consumed-digest parity test over the real v4 train set (CPU) are green.
- **Eta rule at step 10:** with `elapsed` counted from step 0 (not process start), if `elapsed/10 × 200 > 21,600 − 900`, stop with `termination="eta_rule"`. A capped, eta-killed or non-finite run writes a row that says so and is not a parity result; it is rerun, not remembered.
- **Determinism at 2B: not run.** A second Mac run does not fit the window. The loss-log digest (`LossLog.digest()` = sha256 over `[{step, epoch, index, loss_hex}]` [V `run_control.py:762-768`]) is recorded so a later repeat can be compared.

## Pass criteria (written before the run; `quick`, rule 8; nothing promotes)

0. **Gate on the comparison itself:** the consumed digest over the 200 batches is identical for the Rust reader and both torch arms, and every arm's `span_head_init` digest matches. Otherwise no loss comparison is made and the row says `digest_mismatch`.
1. **Per-step combined loss** (letter + 1.0 × span, as `LossPoint.loss`), tessl vs T-fp32: |Δ| ≤ 0.02 nats for steps 0–49; EMA(0.9) of |Δ| ≤ 0.05 for steps 50–199. **Loose bound if only T-bf16 exists:** |Δ| ≤ 0.05 for steps 0–49; EMA ≤ 0.10 after. T-bf16's own |Δ| vs T-fp32 is reported beside tessl's as the yardstick (report-only).
2. **Pre-clip gradient norm per step:** report-only |Δ| (tessl's `grad_sq_norm` + the host head norm vs torch's clip norm); not a pass/fail bound at this rung.
3. **Non-finite loss in any arm:** that arm's row is `killed`; no comparison.
4. **Masters vs base, the criterion that has power at 200 steps:** per parameter group, cosine and norm ratio of Δ = (W_arm − W_base) between the tessl f32 masters and T-fp32's sidecar masters, computed on the **f32 masters, not the bf16 export** (a 200-step delta sits near bf16's resolution). **Where:** on the box, on CPU, after the Mac's 8 GB f32 masters cross with the 4 GB export in the lead's one transfer; the computation is `AUDIT/j7-avg-ood-diag-2026-10-01/delta_cosine.py`'s (main `9715e8b`; argv `sidecars… --avg-manifest --base`) behind a safetensors-master input adapter — throwaway analysis, never ships. **Bound, pre-registered now, relative to torch's own numerics noise:** per group, cos(Δ_tessl, Δ_fp32) ≥ cos(Δ_bf16, Δ_fp32) and |ln(‖Δ_tessl‖/‖Δ_fp32‖)| ≤ |ln(‖Δ_bf16‖/‖Δ_fp32‖)| + 0.01 — tessl (bf16 operands, f32 everything else) must sit no further from fp32 than torch's master-bf16 does — with an absolute sanity floor cos ≥ 0.9 per group. The relative bound governs. If T-fp32 cannot run, (4) is report-only (no yardstick), and the row says so. Rung (b)'s final-weights result (≤1e-5 of each param's max, 20 ExactF32 steps, tiny tower) is not transferable to 200 bf16-operand steps on the 2B and is not used as this bound.
5. **Export loads** in `real_ft_run --score-checkpoint` on the GH200; the `eval` row cites the Mac `ft` row.
6. **Val (low power at this size, stated as such):** |ojas-reduced − T-fp32-reduced| on `val_top1.choice` and `val_top1.span` ≤ F's across-seed range on that metric once F's three rows exist (report-only until then); the same gate verdicts (`permutation_consistency`, `degenerate_head`, `ood_abstain`) on both reduced arms — "the same verdict", not "pass": a 200-step model may fail `degenerate_head` on both sides, as the 352-step Mac run did [V `mac-gpu-lane:43`].

## What the comparison can and cannot show

- **Can:** that the Rust reader, supervision, head, schedule, clip and AdamW compose into torch's training step on the real 2B, the real v4 batches and F's exact recipe, over 200 consecutive steps (200 paired loss points, two paired master deltas); that the export is a scorer-valid artifact; that the gate machinery runs on it. That is the sentence "a Rust trainer over canonical tessl trained Lappi" with a bounded "tracks PyTorch".
- **Cannot:** anything about F's quality or gates at F's step count (the model is 2% trained; both reduced arms sit near base, so the val criterion passes almost by construction); the full cosine (the 200-step cosine is the recipe's formula compressed, identical across arms, not F's trajectory); drift over 9,683 steps (bf16-operand rounding, AdamW moment drift); seeds 1–2; determinism at 2B; MPS-vs-CUDA scoring equivalence (both scorings are on the GH200, which is why they are).
- Promotion still needs three seeds on the full schedule under the same gates: ≈189 h per seed at the floor (9,683 × 70.1 s), ≈570 h for three — a later decision, after (d)'s numbers.
