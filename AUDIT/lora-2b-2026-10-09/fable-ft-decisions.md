## Q0. The framing the lead must carry to the human first

**Decision.** The human's request bundles three things that pull apart: (i) Unsloth's general decision-model recipe (4-bit base, LoRA r=16, seq 2048, a Clef-style head, 2 epochs, warmup 10); (ii) Lappi's own protocol (v5's recipe, 9,638-token rows, the 8K needle gate, 17-row readout, pre-registration, 3 seeds, rule 8); (iii) "close every PyTorch-parity gap in ojas". The ruling that resolves the bundle: **this is a new, separately pre-registered recipe family ("LoRA-2B"), compared with v5 at the gate level only, never row for row; the parity target ojas is held to is a torch arm of the *same* LoRA recipe, not v5's full-fine-tune trainer; and the Mac produces `quick` rows that decide whether box hours are spent, never a promotion.** Rule 2 is untouched by construction: no gate, threshold, seed count or held-out set moves. What the Mac run can honestly claim is the sentence from my 10-01 ruling with one word changed: "a Rust trainer *in ojas* over canonical tessl kernels trained a Lappi LoRA checkpoint on the M5 Pro; the campaign's scorer scored it at X; row `<id>`, quick=True."

**Why this framing and not "follow Unsloth".** Three of Unsloth's defaults are wrong for Lappi on the evidence: 4-bit (Unsloth's own Qwen3.5 page: "It is not recommended to do QLoRA (4-bit) training on the Qwen3.5 models, no matter MoE or dense" [V fetched 2026-10-09]; Lappi's audit already records it [V `AUDIT/training-audit-2026-10-06.md:469`]); seq 2048 (v5's widest trained batch is 9,638 tokens [V ledger row `8079c966`, `recipe.width` 9638, `corpus.plan_max_width` 9638] and the gate reads an 8K needle bucket [V `docs/lappi.md:104`, `README.md:27`]); and a new hidden-state head (Lappi's serving, calibration and gates are built on the tied-embedding 17-row readout [V `crates/qd-metal/src/model.rs:23` `score_answer_rows(..., embed[answer ids])`]). The rest of Unsloth's recipe (bf16 LoRA r=16 α=16 dropout 0 on all linears, lr 2e-4, wd 0.01, cosine, checkpointing, adapters + merged export) is sound and is adopted.

**Why "parity target = torch arm of the same recipe".** No PyTorch reference trains LoRA in Lappi today (`backbone.py:1186-1187` full FT [R audit-lappi §2.2]; v0.1 README: "AdamW with fp32 master weights, learning rate 1e-5, lower 8 layers at 0.1x, one epoch (12,176 steps), five seeds averaged" [V `README.md:133-134`]). A LoRA ojas run cannot be "at parity" with a full-FT trainer; it can be at parity with a plain-torch LoRA arm on the same data, init and batch order (D7).

## D1. Path: LoRA/frozen base on the Tape; the provider stays the full-FT path with small fixes

**Ruling: Tape canonical for frozen base + LoRA; provider (ojas-qwen35 over tessl's fused step) keeps full fine-tune and gets only the correctness fixes in `gp-ft-training-path-correctness` plus the `Precision::Bf16` passthrough. Mine to rule (architecture), with one gating run before any LoRA code is written (below). The human had provisionally decided the same [V `tasks/gp-ft-frozen-params-and-lora.md` "Path decision (provisional, the user's)"].**

**Rationale.**
- The Tape has the whole hybrid tower forward and backward on CPU and Metal since `66c5e52` ("Qwen3.5 hybrid tower on Tape, sigmoid and GDN decay ops, per-group LR", 2026-10-08) and `b363802` ("Qwen3.5 hybrid-layer ops on tessl kernels") [V `git log`], with per-layer activation checkpointing (`forward.rs:181-189` `g.checkpoint(|g| layer(...))`) [V]. The op math was checked formula by formula against transformers by three independent reads and found correct [R audit-bugs "Bottom line"; I did not redo it].
- LoRA on the Tape is composition, not kernel work: two small f32 leaves and existing `linear`/`add` ops; what is missing is a frozen-leaf concept (`Tape::leaf` records every leaf as `Rec::Leaf`, `tape.rs:362-365`; the walk retains a gradient for every leaf, `tape.rs:1040-1042`; `Rec::Linear` backward always calls `linear_backward` and accumulates `gw`, `tape.rs:1134-1139`; the trait returns `(grad_input, grad_weight)` unconditionally, `ojas-core/src/backend.rs:654-659`) [V]. That is one `Backend` method (`linear_backward_input`) with CPU and Metal bodies — both GEMMs tessl already has — not a new kernel.
- LoRA inside tessl's fused step would be Qwen3.5-only, Metal-only, and would re-plumb every one of `qwen35_train.rs`/`qwen35_bwd.rs`'s per-layer rebuilds for a second weight path; tessl has no LoRA at all [V `rg -uu` 0 hits]. It would also leave the recipe on the path that steps **one sequence per launch** with a host round-trip of hidden rows per sequence (`ojas-qwen35/src/step.rs` module docs; `step.rs:462-474, 523-528` [R audit-model-arch §1b; V the module doc at `step.rs:330-339` for the four-copy claim]).
- The provider's "freeze" is `lr_scale = 0` whose moments still update (`step.rs:584-586` doc: "a scale of 0 freezes the entry while its moments still update") and whose gradient still enters `grad_sq_norm` over the whole bank (`step.rs:572-578`) [V]; four f32 copies per parameter, 30.1 GB at 2B (`step.rs:333-336`) [V]. Making that a real frozen base is tessl work (bank sized to trainable entries) for no gain the Tape does not already give.

**Reconciliation with my 2026-10-01 ruling.** That ruling said "ojas today cannot express Qwen3.5" and framed the work as "what ojas owns above tessl" through a step-provider trait that tessl's `Qwen35Model` implements first and "ojas's Tape model implements later" [V `fable-advice.md` Q0]. The human recorded exactly that: "tessl's `Qwen35Model` is the first provider, and ojas's Tape model a later one" [V `HANDOFF/ojas-training-2026-10-01.md:25`]. The premise "ojas cannot express Qwen3.5" ended at `66c5e52`; the trait decision does not end — it is the seam D1 and D8 use: the Tape tower becomes the second `StepProvider`. Rule 6 is intact: ojas-metal's hybrid ops *call tessl kernels* (`b363802`), so the kernel owner is unchanged. What **does not hold any more** from 10-01: "nothing in rows 1–7, 9, 11 is built; it is wired" was about tessl; for ojas, rows 1–7 and 11 are now built on the Tape too, so there are two Qwen3.5 training paths and the audits are right that this is a duplication to manage (R1 "two towers, two name maps" → `gp-qwen35-host-neutral-crate`), not a reason to pick the provider.

**The gate before LoRA code (the first GPU job of this track).** `ojas-qwen35/tests/gpu_tape_2b.rs` — the real 2B through the Metal Tape — has never run [V `tasks/gp-long-runs-and-quiet-benches.md:57` per audit; V the brief `gp-qwen35-2b-metal-tape-run` status ready]. It can run **today without a download**: `models--Qwen--Qwen3.5-2B` (instruct) holds a 4.3 GB single-shard snapshot with the same text config as 2B-Base [V `ls`/`du` of the HF cache; V both configs: hidden 2048, 24 layers, GDN 16/16, attn 8/2, head_dim 256, vocab 248,320]. Run it at T=128 against the provider (its own bound: loss ≤1e-4 rel, grads ≤1e-2 of peak [R audit-model-arch GAP-7]), then one forward+backward at T=2048 and at T=9,638, micro-batch 1, checkpointing on, with peak device bytes recorded. **Reversal condition:** the Tape fails that parity bound on real weights, or after `gp-ft-step-memory-and-throughput`'s P1–P3 items its tokens/s at T=2048 is below half the provider's on the same run — then LoRA goes into tessl's fused step and this ruling is reversed in writing.

## D2. Head: Lappi's 17-row readout kept, trained as a 17-way CE with the rows as a trainable delta; no Clef head for this model

**Ruling: the new model keeps Lappi's served interface — a softmax over the 17 answer rows (up to 16 letters + noul) of the tied embedding — and trains it as a 17-way cross-entropy over exactly those rows at the answer position, with the 17 rows carried as a trainable f32 delta `D17 [17, H]` (init 0) over the frozen embedding rows, at the head learning rate. The span pointer head stays as it is (host f32, pinned init). The Unsloth/Clef hidden-state option head is not built for this model. This changes the training objective of the model class, so it is the human's decision (H1); my recommendation is firm.**

**Rationale.**
- With a frozen base the tied embedding cannot move, so "a head at 1e-4" must be *something* new in both designs; the smallest something that keeps serving unchanged is a 17-row delta: it is a Clef-style linear head over the final hidden state with a very good initialisation (the base's own letter rows). Serving reads `embed[answer ids] + D17` instead of `embed[answer ids]` — an additive change to `qd-metal`'s `score_answer_rows` input and to `qd-export`'s layout, no new op [I from `model.rs:23`].
- v0.1 trained a **248,320-way** CE at the answer position (`fused_linear_cross_entropy(hidden[:, :-1], lm_head_weight, targets, mask)`, `backbone.py:1273-1281` [V]) but serves a 17-way softmax; the audit is right that the 17-way objective removes a train/serve mismatch Lappi carries today [R audit-lappi §5; I agree]. Unsloth's recipe is "CE over options" — the 17-way objective is that recipe on Lappi's option encoding.
- A true Clef head (third-party descriptions: a small transformer head reading final hidden states, one logit per option per question, softmax per question, label-smoothed CE + Brier, LoRA r=256 on 27B/9B) [R web search summaries; Cloudflare's own page at blog.cloudflare.com/clef returned 404] solves multi-question joint decisions and letter-free options. Lappi is one decision per prompt with letters in the prompt; a new head changes `qd-runtime`, `qd-metal`, calibration and every gate's model class with no Lappi evidence that it helps. It is a rung-4 candidate for the ladder, not this run.
- Cost is not the discriminator: with a frozen embedding, full-vocab CE at K answer positions is K × 248,320 × 2,048 — trivial — and produces no dense dW. So the choice is purely the objective. **Report-only:** log the full-vocab letter CE at the same positions (no gradient) so the v0.1 "letter floor" number exists on every LoRA row.

**Reversal condition.** The 60-step smoke runs both objectives (17-way vs full-vocab, same seed and batch order; cheap). If full-vocab beats 17-way on `val_top1.choice` by more than 2 points *and* gives a lower in-distribution abstention count, the pre-registration names full-vocab instead. Either way the pre-registration is written before the smoke is read.

## D3. Precision: bf16 frozen base + f32 LoRA/head/norms; 8- and 4-bit not built now, gated on a measurement with a stated bar

**Ruling: 16-bit. The frozen base is resident as bf16 (2 B/elem), GEMM operands bf16 with f32 accumulation, f32 residual stream, LoRA/head/norm parameters and optimizer state f32. Neither 8-bit nor 4-bit is built in this track. Mine to rule; the human asked for "16 or 8 or 4", so this is reported to them with the gate below.**

**Rationale.**
- Unsloth: QLoRA not recommended on any Qwen3.5 ("higher than normal quantization differences"); its bf16 LoRA path is `load_in_4bit = False, load_in_16bit = True`; VRAM 0.8B 3 GB, 2B 5 GB, 4B 10 GB [V fetched]. Lappi's audit: "Avoid QLoRA on Qwen3.5" [V `training-audit-2026-10-06.md:469`].
- Memory does not force quantisation on this machine. 2B bf16 base ≈ 3.8 GB; activations at B=8×2048 with per-layer checkpointing ≈ 8–9 GB [I, audit-model-arch §5 arithmetic]; LoRA state negligible; against a Metal working set tessl probed at 51.54 GB [V `HANDOFF/ojas-training-2026-10-01.md:101-105` via audit-lappi §1.3]. Even the 4B's bf16 base (≈8.4 GB) plus ≈14–17 GB activations fits [I]. The only remaining argument for 4-bit is "match `load_in_4bit`", which Unsloth itself withdraws for Qwen3.5.
- What exists: tessl trains a bf16-stored model (`Precision::Bf16`, `qwen35_model.rs:389-392`; the train step requires `GemmOperands::Bf16` for it, `qwen35_train.rs:715-718`; AdamW `UpdateRule::{F32Master, Bf16Kahan, Bf16Stochastic}` for bf16 models, `qwen35_adamw.rs:248-250`; `docs/qwen35.md:1136-1147` describes it) [V]; ojas-qwen35 hard-codes `Precision::F32` (`step.rs:361`) [V] — the passthrough is the cheapest memory win on the provider. On the Metal Tape, bf16 operands are refused for linear fwd/bwd and SDPA fwd/bwd (`ojas-metal/src/backend.rs:1257, 1283, 1427, 1443` `refuse_bf16_operands`) [V]; the fine-tune needs exactly those four plus `linear_backward_input` — **not** the whole `gp-bf16-compute-tier` (see priorities).
- The quantised path's true cost: no int4/int8/NF4 dtype (`DType = F32|Bf16|F16|U32`), tessl's q4/q8 kernels are GEMV or M≤8 with no transposed variant for dX, gemma-metal's host quantiser is in a separate workspace with three bugs [R audit-quant-lora §2, B2–B4; V `ojas-io` dtype set `StDtype` F32/BF16/F16/I64/U16 per audit, not re-read]. That is weeks of tessl kernel work for a recipe the model's authors say degrades.

**The gate (if the human still wants 8/4-bit).** A Lappi-side measurement, not an ojas task: quantise the bf16 2B-Base with `mlx.core.quantize` (mlx 0.31.2 installed [V import]) to affine q8 g64 and q4 g64, score ≥ 500 Lappi **validation** prompts (never held-out; rule 3) with the letter log-softmax over the 17 rows in bf16 vs q8 vs q4. **Bar, written before the run:** argmax agreement ≥ 99.0% and mean |Δ log-prob| on the gold letter ≤ 0.02 nats for a format to be *admitted as an option*; and a demonstrated memory need (a shape that does not fit in bf16 at micro-batch 1) before it is *built*. Record the numbers in `docs/fine-tune-plan.md`. **Reversal:** q8 clears the bar *and* the chosen base does not fit in bf16 → 8-bit storage with dX-only dequant GEMM becomes the unblocking item (tessl kernels, rule 6). 4-bit needs the same plus the human overriding Unsloth's advice in writing.

## D5. Release form and seed averaging: merged bf16 dense tower + head tensors; adapters are the training artifact; average merged weights only

**Ruling: the release is a merged dense bf16 tower (`W' = W + (α/r)·B·A` computed in f32, rounded to bf16 once, RNE), plus `D17` (f32 or bf16, documented) and `span_head.safetensors` (f32), through `ckpt_average` → `qd-export` → `qd-metal` with one additive change (the head rows). Adapters (+ head) are the checkpoint and resume unit and are also exported in PEFT's format for the reference arm's round-trip test; adapters are not served. Seed averaging averages merged weights (equivalently merged deltas, since the base is shared); averaging A and B separately is refused with a typed error. Mine to rule; consistent with the recorded decisions.**

**Rationale.**
- `qd-metal` reads dense BF16/F16/F32 only; no quantised or adapter path exists [R audit-lappi §4.2]. `qd-export` writes `model.safetensors` (bf16 RNE) + `span_head.safetensors` + manifest [R §4.1]. With D3 (bf16 base, no quantisation) the merge target question ("dequant(Q(W)) or W?") does not arise — the merge is into the very bf16 `W` the adapters trained over; record that in the manifest anyway (`gp-ft-export` AC3 stays, as a test that the base hash is recorded).
- `ckpt_average --from masters` averages fp32 masters in float64 and casts to bf16 once, weights only, `"resumable": false` [V `tools/ckpt_average.py:36-39, 28`]. For LoRA the "masters" are A, B, D17 and the span head; the tool must be taught to average `W'` (or `(α/r)·B·A`), not A and B — `mean(B)·mean(A) ≠ mean(B·A)` [I, elementary]. Lappi-side item L4.
- `average_may_promote: false` stands (the human, 2026-10-03, through my ruling) [V `docs/promotion-decisions.json`]: the seeds are the promoted thing; the average is the release artifact. Calibration is fit on the averaged model as v5 did [R audit-lappi §2.11].
- PEFT format, verified today from the peft source: `SAFETENSORS_WEIGHTS_NAME = "adapter_model.safetensors"`, `CONFIG_NAME = "adapter_config.json"` [V fetched `peft/utils/constants.py`]; `lora_A = nn.Linear(in_features, r)`, `lora_B = nn.Linear(r, out_features)`; init `kaiming_uniform_(lora_A.weight, a=math.sqrt(5))`, `zeros_(lora_B.weight)`; `scaling = lora_alpha / r` (rsLoRA: `/ sqrt(r)`) [V fetched `peft/tuners/lora/layer.py`]. The key prefix (`base_model.model.<module>.lora_A.weight`) and `adapter_config.json` fields remain [U] until read from the pinned peft source; the brief says so and is right.

**Reversal condition.** If the human later chooses to serve adapters on a quantised base (D3 reversed), this ruling is reopened with the serving work (L5) costed first.

## D6. Recipe and comparability with v5: a new pre-registration; what it says

**Ruling: every knob below breaks row-level comparability with v0.1/v5, and that is accepted: the LoRA-2B family is pre-registered as its own recipe in `campaign/` (precaution 1), with `optimizer_recipe = "lora"` (a new value, so precaution 5's filter never pools it with `master`) and compared with v5 only at the gates (same gates, same thresholds, same held-out and val sets — rule 2). The pre-registration's content is the human's decision (H4); below are my recommendations, each with its reason.**
