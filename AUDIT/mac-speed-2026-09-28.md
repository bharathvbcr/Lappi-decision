## 2. How far from the hardware that is

FLOPs per token, from `config.json` [I]:

| --- | --- |
| linear layers (≈1.37B non-embedding params × 2) | 2.74 |
| **full-vocab lm_head** (248,320 × 2048 × 2), computed because the bench passes `labels=` | **1.02** |
| attention (6 layers, T≈1–2k) | ~0.05 |
| GDN recurrence (18 layers) | ~0.03 |
| **total** | **≈3.8** |

At 870 tok/s that is **≈3.3 TFLOP/s achieved**. tessl measured **26.6 TFLOP/s** bf16 through MPP
TensorOps on this chip (`tessl/README.md:146`) [V]. So the forward runs at **~12% of the chip's
demonstrated matmul rate** [I]. If the GEMMs alone ran at half that peak, they would take ~0.3 s of
the 1.19 s step; **~0.9 s per 1k-token forward is something other than useful matmul** [I].

## 3. Where the time goes — ranked by expected size

### 3.1 The GDN is a dispatch storm, not a compute load [V code, I cost]

`torch_chunk_gated_delta_rule` (`modeling_qwen3_5.py:246-331`) does almost no math — ~0.03
GFLOP/token — but issues it as eager ops:

- **The intra-chunk triangular solve is a 63-iteration Python loop** (`:292-295`): per iteration a
  slice, two `clone()`s, a broadcast multiply, a `sum`, an add and a slice-assign — roughly **6–8
  MPS dispatches × 63 ≈ 400–500 per layer**, regardless of sequence length.
- **The inter-chunk recurrence is a second Python loop** over `T/64` chunks (`:306-316`), ~12 ops
  each: 16 chunks at 1k → ~190 dispatches, 32 at 2k → ~380.
- Setup: 5 transposes + `.contiguous()` + fp32 upcasts, 5 pads, cumsum, decay mask, `eye`, 2 masks.

So **~700–900 dispatches per GDN layer × 18 layers ≈ 13–16k tiny kernel launches per forward**, on
tensors of a few KB each [I]. MPS eager dispatch costs tens of µs each, and the GPU idles between
them. This is the dominant cost on the Mac, and on CUDA fla replaces it with a handful of fused
kernels — which is why the GH200 does not see it.

Everything in it runs **fp32** (`:263`, `.to(torch.float32)`), so none of it touches the bf16
TensorOps path.

### 3.2 The full-vocab lm_head — a benchmark and training artefact, not an inference cost [V code, I size]

`labels=x` makes HF compute logits for every position over **248,320** tokens and a full-vocab
cross-entropy: 27% of forward FLOPs, plus a 1 GB (1k) / 2 GB (2k) fp32 logits tensor and its
softmax. **Decision inference needs none of it** — only the 17-row letter slice, and only at slot
positions (`README.md`, `schema.rs` `RESERVED_NOUL_ROWS`). For Lappi's real workload this term goes
to ≈0. The measured 870 tok/s therefore **understates** what even torch would do on the real task.

### 3.3 Backward makes 3.1 much worse — why full FT collapses at 2k [I]

Autograd records every op in both loops. The 63-step solve clones a 64×64 block per iteration, so it
saves ~63 intermediates per chunk per layer for backward. That explains:
- full FT at 32 GB for 1k tokens of batch 1, 48 GB at 2k;
- **mean 26.7 s vs min 13.6 s at 2k** — a 2× spread is not compute; it is memory pressure (48 of
  64 GB, plus the OS) compressing/swapping [I];
- batch 4 at 1k reaching 77 GB with no timed step [V].

This is a training-on-Mac problem. The user trains on the GH200, so it is recorded, not targeted.

### 3.4 Smaller terms [I/U]

- `nn.Conv1d` depthwise k=4 over 6,144 channels, then SiLU — separate ops, plus layout transposes.
- q/k l2norm, gate prologue (`g = −exp(A_log)·softplus(a+dt_bias)`), `sigmoid(b)`, gated RMSNorm ×
  `silu(z)`, attention output `sigmoid` gate, partial RoPE — each several elementwise dispatches.
- SDPA at head_dim 256 on MPS: whether it takes a fused path or materialises T×T is **[U]**.
- Whether PyTorch's MPS bf16 matmul uses the M5's TensorOps / neural accelerators at all is **[U]** —
  tessl's 26.6 TFLOP/s came from MPP `matmul2d`; torch goes through MPSGraph.

## 4. What this means for tessl — the kernel list, in order of payoff

The lesson of §3: **on this chip the model is launch-bound and fp32-bound in the GDN, not
FLOP-bound.** tessl's design — one command buffer per forward, bump-allocated arenas, no host
waits (`tessl/README.md` "Zero-Wait Execution") — already removes eager dispatch overhead for
whatever it runs. The kernels that matter:

| # | Kernel | Replaces | Why it pays | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | **Fused chunked GDN forward (K1)** | ~700–900 dispatches/layer | largest single win; GDN is 18 of 24 layers | bf16 q/k/v in, fp32 state. The 64×64 fp32 solve block is 16 KB — fits the **32 KB threadgroup limit** measured in `AUDIT/tessl-integration.md` §B.5, so the triangular solve runs in threadgroup memory, not 63 dispatches. The 128×128 fp32 state is 64 KB — split along Dv across ≥2 threadgroups (e.g. 4 × 32 columns = 16 KB each). Intra-chunk (WY/UT) phase parallel over chunks; inter-chunk state pass sequential. Reference structure: decider `mps_ops.py:136-221` (Apache) and mlx_lm `gated_delta.py` (MIT) |
| 2 | **GDN prologue fused into K1's load** (K7 gates + l2norm + q·1/√128 + β) | ~10 elementwise ops/layer | free once #1 exists | gate formula per `GAP-K7-GOLDEN-PINS-NANOLAB-SIGMOID-NOT-QWEN35-GATE` — fix the golden first |
| 3 | **Causal conv1d k=4 + SiLU**, fused | conv + transposes + activation | small kernel, 18 layers | conv state (3 × 6,144) kept for decode |
| 4 | **Gated RMSNorm × silu(z)** fused | 4–5 ops/layer | 18 layers | plain `w` (not 1+w) in this norm |
| 5 | **Readonly GDN decode (K2)** from snapshot | per-slot recurrent step | Lappi answers many slots per prefill | broadcast state (stride 0), no store; runtime hash check |
| 6 | **Attention epilogue:** partial RoPE (first 64 dims, p↔p+32, θ=1e7) + q/k norm + output `sigmoid` gate | existing `rms_qkv_rope` does not match (V-norm, full-D pairing) | 6 layers | new variant, not a flag |
| 7 | **17-row lm_head at slot positions only** | full-vocab logits | removes 27% of FLOPs of a full-logits forward | a gather + tiny GEMM |
| 8 | **Projection fusion** (qkvz / ba concatenated GEMMs; fused epilogues) | several GEMM launches/layer | tessl's fused-epilogue GEMM is measured 1.6–2.4× cheaper than a separate pass | uses existing `gemm` + epilogue |

With #1–#4 in place the forward is GEMM-bound, and the ceiling is the arithmetic in the plan:
≈2.75 GFLOP/token at 26.6 TFLOP/s → ~9.7k tok/s peak, 4–6k realistic [I] — **5–7× the 870 tok/s
torch baseline**, before prefix reuse.

**Training kernels on tessl** (a backward for K1, and autograd integration) would be a separate,
much larger project. fla's backward is the reference. Not recommended while the GH200 does training.

## 5. The measurement that confirms this — run once, plugged in, ~15 min

The ranking in §3 is inferred. Before writing kernel #1, attribute the 1.19 s:

1. **Dispatch count and GPU idle:** `torch.mps.profiler.profile(mode="interval", wait_until_completed=False)`
   around one forward, opened in Instruments (Metal System Trace). Count kernels per step; measure
   GPU-busy fraction. Prediction: >10k kernels, GPU busy well under 50%.
2. **GDN share:** time one forward with the 18 GDN mixers replaced by identity (shapes preserved).
   Prediction: tok/s rises 2–4×.
3. **lm_head share:** forward with `labels=None`, reading only the last position's 17 letter rows.
   Prediction: ~25% faster.
4. **torch bf16 matmul rate:** a 4096×2048×6144 bf16 matmul loop on MPS vs tessl's `gemm` on the same
   shape. Settles §3.4's last [U] and tells kernel #8 how much headroom torch left.
5. **SDPA h256:** 6-layer attention alone at T=1k/2k/8k, memory growth vs T — settles fused vs T×T.

Each result goes into a ledger row. If (2) shows GDN under ~30% of the step, kernel #1 moves down
the list and #8 (GEMM efficiency) moves up.

What this changes:

- **The ranking holds.** GDN is the dominant cost, then the full-vocab head. The fused GDN kernel
  (#1) was the right first kernel; GEMM tuning (#8) has little headroom on torch's side.
- **§1's 1.19 s per 1k-token forward is 2.7× slower than today's 0.439 s for the same call.** The
  cause is unverified. §1 ran **on battery** and today's run was on AC, so power-state throttling is
  the leading explanation [I]. §2's "12% of peak" is therefore pessimistic; use the table above.
- **MLX already reaches ~7.6k tok/s** with its own fused GDN. That is the bar tessl's forward has to
  clear to be worth its complexity, and it is above §4's 4–6k estimate.
- **torch bf16 on MPS is numerically off for letter logits** (`GAP-TORCH-MPS-BF16-LETTER-LOGITS-DRIFT`).
  Against torch fp32: MLX fp32 differs by ≤0.012 (50/50 argmax), MLX bf16 by ≤0.078 (49/50), and
  torch bf16 by ~0.8 (45–46/50). Any Mac-side eval scored through torch bf16 is suspect; score in
  fp32 or through MLX. The op responsible is not localised.

Where the time goes:

- **At 8k, `flash_attn_rows` takes about 1.0 s of the 2.0 s forward** (6 layers × ~169 ms) and runs
  at ~1.6 TFLOP/s against the GEMMs' ~25. The scalar-f32 attention is the largest lever for long
  prefills, which is Lappi's main workload (DevCouncil relevance is ~8–12k tokens of shared prefix).
  The GDN chunk forward is 18 × 9 ms = 8%.
- **At 1k the forward is GEMM-bound:** the GEMMs take ~110 of 168 ms and already run at
  ~25 TFLOP/s. The GDN chunk forward is ~15%, flash attention 14 ms, SwiGLU + cast 12 ms.
- Launch count is solved: 307 launches per forward, against transformers' ~13–16k (§3.1).

The optimization order agreed with tessl: tiled bf16 prefill attention at h256 / GQA 8:2 first, then
the GDN chunk forward, then SwiGLU + cast fusion. Each is gated on this bench at pinned commits. The
target is MLX's 5,285 tok/s at 8k.
