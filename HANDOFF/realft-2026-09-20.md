# REALFT lane — 2026-09-20

**`train_ft` trains on real shards.** The FT lane proved S4's loop executes on a toy
vocabulary over twelve rows built inside its own tool, and named what was left: *"the same
treatment with real shards/tokenizer/remap."* This lane did that. The corpus is the one
`tools/real_tokenizer_pipeline.py` writes with the live `Qwen/Qwen3.5-2B-Base` tokenizer —
321 sequences, 2,485,641 tokens, a 13,787-token remapped vocabulary, lengths 320 to 34,522 —
read back through the real `ShardReader` and driven through the real `qd_train.trainer.train_ft`.

**And the corpus does not say what everyone assumed it says.** Two measurements, both
first-time, both free, both of which an 8xH100 run would have paid for and not detected:

* **Every one of the 78 span rows is one half of a prompt-identical pair with a
  contradictory gold.** 78 of 78, in 39 groups. The span channel's entropy floor is
  therefore `ln 2` exactly, and `train_ft` converges to it. 20 or 21 of 39 abstentions decode
  as abstain, by seed — which is the coin flip that floor predicts, not a layout defect.
* **Not one of the 243 letter rows has `noul` as its gold.** The abstain row of a `choice`
  or `score` slot is in the vocabulary, reachable at serve time, and supervised zero times —
  and cross-entropy on the other letters trains the model *against* it.

**And two more, from asking the same question of the tooling rather than the corpus.** Both
came out of runs that had to be repeated, which is how they were visible at all:

* **A seed does not pin an `mps` run.** Re-running the identical command left all three `cpu`
  seeds bit-identical — `train.loss_log_digest`, a hash of the whole loss log — and changed
  all three `mps` seeds. On one of them it changed a reported answer: 21 of 39 abstentions
  decoded correctly, then 20 of 39. Section 3.
* **A channel with no rows in the plan was skipped by accident rather than on purpose.**
  `nan > FLOOR_SLACK` is `False`, so this lane's own tool put nothing in its failure list for
  a channel whose ledger row said `passed=False` — and wrote four bare `NaN` tokens into an
  append-only ledger while it was there. One command-line flag away. Section 6.6.

**Every check that could refuse this corpus passed it.** `assert_shard_trainable`
(`artifacts.py:870`) returns five checks all `passed=True`, and `ShardReader.checks` holds a

Nothing here promotes anything: every row is `quick`, and rule 8 excludes it. Nothing was

## 3. The runs

Two arms, because one run cannot answer both questions this lane was given. **Arm 1** is one
full real epoch — every batch the shard set produces, in the order `ShardReader.batches`
produces it — and answers *does `train_ft` consume a real shard set end to end*. It cannot
answer *does the loss reach the floor*, because one pass over 321 sequences is 106 optimizer
steps. **Arm 2** repeats a bounded, deterministically-chosen subset of the same real batches
until the loss stops moving, and answers the second question against the corpus's own entropy
floor. Both are `quick` under rule 8 and promote nothing; the `quick_reason` on every row
says which truncation it is.

**Arm 2's subset is chosen by width, not by content.** `--max-width 2947` keeps every real
batch at most 2,947 positions wide: **5 of 106 batches, 81 of 321 sequences**. Those 81 are
78 span rows and 3 letter rows — not because span rows were selected for, but because the
corpus's span rows are 320 to 1,514 tokens long and its letter rows start at 1,359, so the
two narrow buckets are almost entirely span. That is the honest reading of arm 2: **it is a
span experiment with three letter rows attached**, and `letter_loss_reached_its_floor` carries
`n=3` on every row rather than implying it tested the letter channel.

## 4. Where the abstention decoded, for every slot kind present

First-hand: `answer.rs:303, 366, 398, 482` are `let noul_row = plan.rows -
RESERVED_NOUL_ROWS;`, `schema.rs:195-196` size a choice slot at `options.len() +
RESERVED_NOUL_ROWS` and a score slot at `bins + RESERVED_NOUL_ROWS`, `answer.rs:70` sizes a
span slot at `context.line_count() + RESERVED_NOUL_ROWS`, and `schema.rs:112` is

| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `choice` | 3 (2 options + noul) | `QueryKind::Letters`: `lm_head` logits at this slot's rendered letters, argmax | **0 of 123** | **NOT RUN** |
| `score` | 6 (5 bins + noul) | the same letter slice — **plain cross-entropy, no ordinal loss** | **0 of 120** | **NOT RUN** |
| `span` | `line_count + 1`, 15 to 61 | `PointerStart`/`PointerEnd` through `heads.serving_scores` | 39 of 78 | `cpu` 20/39, 20/39, 21/39; `mps` 20/39, 20/39, 20/39 |

**The span abstention is exactly where `answer.rs` reads it, on real data.** For all 78 rows
the head's abstain column is `n_candidates[k]`, asserted per row in the decode; and
`_check_runtime_rows` — which refuses any row whose finite columns are not exactly
`0..n_candidates[k]` — runs inside `SpanPointerHead.forward` *and* again inside
`serving_scores`, so it fired on every span row of every step of every run and never refused.
Real candidate sets of 14 to 60 line starts. That join had never been exercised outside a
byte-level fixture.

**20 or 21 of 39 is the corpus, not the layout.** Every abstaining span row has a
prompt-identical twin with a concrete gold, so the model splits its mass and the argmax is a
coin flip; `prefix_identical_rows_decode_identically` is 39 of 39 with 0 inconsistencies,
which proves the two members get one verdict between them. Section 5.1. How thin the coin is
has a second demonstration in section 3: re-running one `mps` seed moved it from 21 to 20.

**The letter-channel abstention is NOT RUN, and that is the finding.** The corpus holds no
`choice` or `score` row whose gold is `noul`, so "0 of 0 abstentions decoded correctly" is
not a pass and is not recorded as one. Section 5.2.

**The score slot got plain cross-entropy through `fused_linear_cross_entropy`, the same loss
the choice slot got.** No CORAL, no cumulative, no ordinal anything, because none exists on
either side — `GAP-TRAINER-SCORE-SLOT-HAS-NO-ORDINAL-LOSS`, still open, still not this lane's
to implement. 120 real score rows over 5 ordinal bins were trained that way here.

## 5. The two *corpus* findings that cost nothing here and everything on a rented GPU

(The two tooling findings are in sections 3 and 6.6.)

Both are things that **silently succeed where they should refuse**. Neither is a bug in this
repository's code; both are properties of the corpus that every artifact-level check admits
without comment.

### 5.1 Every span row is half of a contradictory pair — `GAP-REALFT-CONTRADICTORY-SPAN-SUPERVISION`

All **78 of 78** span rows sit in **39 groups of two** whose prompts are byte-identical up to
`target_index` and whose golds contradict. Not a sample — the whole span channel.

The cause is in `tools/real_tokenizer_pipeline.py:span_rows`, which emits an answerable and
an unanswerable `SquadRow` per Markdown file with the same passage **and the same question
text** — `question=f"Which line of {name} states the rule?"` for both — so
`qd_data.mixture.rewrite_squad` builds one row with a concrete `(start_line, end_line)` gold
and one with `is_noul=True` behind identical prompts. Real SQuAD 2.0 gives the unanswerable
question different words; this generator does not.

A causal model conditions on `tokens[:target_index + 1]` and nothing else, so it cannot tell
them apart and must split its mass. The consequences, all measured:

* the plan-level pointer entropy is **0.6931471805599453**, which is `ln 2` exactly;
* `train_ft` converges to it on all three seeds and both devices — the count-weighted span
  loss over the whole plan is **0.702911** against a floor of **0.693147**, a gap of
  **0.009764** against a bar of `FLOOR_SLACK = 0.05` (section 3 has it per seed). The last
  logged *batch* loss is ~0.767 and is a different quantity; comparing that one against the
  plan floor is the mistake this handoff made once and corrected;
* **20 of 39** abstaining rows decode as abstain on seeds 0 and 1, **21 of 39** on seed 2 —
  the coin flip that floor predicts, moving with the seed as a coin flip should;
* `prefix_identical_rows_decode_identically` is **39 of 39 with 0 inconsistencies**, which is
  the proof: a deterministic model on identical input produces one verdict per group, so at
  most one member of each group can ever be right. **The corpus caps span accuracy at 39 of
  78, permanently.**

**`dedupe` sees these pairs and keeps them, deliberately — measured, not read.** Running
`dedupe` over the 608 mixture rows:

```
rows in 608  units 231  kept 608
dropped rows 0  dropped units 0
confirmed pairs crossing a repo boundary: 0
confirmed pairs WITHIN one repo (reported, kept): 75
qa.answer_span rows: 41 answerable, 41 unanswerable
pairs whose dedupe_text is byte-identical: 41 of 41
pairs sharing a repo_key:                  41 of 41
of those pairs, both members survived:     41 of 41
```

(41 pairs in the mixture; 39 of them land in the train split, which is the 39 groups the
shard set holds.) `dedupe_text` is `f"{question}\n{passage}"` and therefore identical, so
exact Jaccard is 1.0 and both rows carry `repo_key="squad-title:<file>"`. `qd_data/dedupe.py`
then takes this branch:

> *"Reported, not merged: same repo means same side of the split, so this pair cannot leak,
> and deleting it would thin the mixture for nothing."*

**That reasoning is about contamination and is correct.** It is silent about trainability.
The number it does keep is in the report — *"75 confirmed pairs were within one repo and
were kept"* — and **nothing downstream reads it as a supervision signal**. The pipeline run
prints `rows in: 608   kept: 608` and leaves no other trace.

On a rented GPU the only symptom is a span loss that plateaus at 0.693 and an eval that
reports 50%, which under rule 5 is a number nobody could attribute. Not fixed here:
`span_rows()` is the PIPELINE lane's, and a dedupe policy change is a plan decision.
`tools/real_ft_run.py` measures it on every run and compares the loss against the plan floor
rather than against zero, so a run on this corpus is legible instead of looking broken.

### 5.2 The letter channel's abstention is never supervised — `GAP-REALFT-LETTER-ABSTENTION-NEVER-SUPERVISED`

**0 of 243** letter rows have `noul` as their gold. Every `noul` in the shard set is the
structural trailing token of a span row (78 of them), and `ft_supervision` routes exactly
those *out* of the letter channel — so `noul` appears in the corpus 78 times and is
supervised 0 times.

Structural twice over, not an accident of sampling:

* `DataConfig.held_out_families` reserves `code.language_id` and `qa.answerability` as rule
  3's task holdout, so the trainable mixture is `code.commit_intent`, `code.change_scope`
  and `qa.answer_span` — which is exactly what the per-kind family counts in section 2 show;
* of the five families the two sources produce, only `qa.answer_span` ever emits a `noul`
  gold, and it is a span slot. `qa.answerability` turns an unanswerable question into gold
  `"no"`, not into an abstention, and is held out in any case;
* the one family that would put an abstention in the letter channel is
  `intent.classification` on `clinc/clinc_oos`, which is not in this corpus — and the HF
  datasets-server has been answering HTTP 500 all day.

**The corpus does not merely fail to teach the abstention; it trains against it.** After 200
passes of the real `train_ft`, the mean softmax mass on the abstain row — over each slot's own
rendered rows, which is exactly what `QueryKind::Letters` decodes — is:

| seed | choice | score |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0 | 9e-08 | 1.6e-07 |
| 1 | 4e-08 | 5e-08 |
| 2 | 2.5e-07 | 1.3e-07 |

Cross-entropy on the other letters drives every non-gold token down, and `noul` is a non-gold
token in every letter row of this corpus. **Caveat, stated:** the backbone is an 8.2M-parameter
randomly-initialised stand-in, so the *magnitude* is not a statement about a 2B checkpoint.
The *direction* is architecture-independent — it is what the objective on this corpus does to
that row.

The FT lane's toy corpus carried one abstaining row per slot kind deliberately, because *"a
corpus of only-abstaining or only-answering rows would let a head that always abstains, or
never does, reach a floor that reads as learning."* The real corpus is that degenerate case
and nothing counts it. `noul_rate` exists in the ledger row and in `docs/ledger-schema.md`,
and every tool in the repository sets it to `NotRun`; it is an eval-time property of a model
in any case, not a train-time property of a corpus.

Not fixed: what fraction of the mixture must carry a letter-channel abstention is a plan
decision, and the source that supplies one is unreachable while the datasets-server is down.
`tools/real_ft_run.py` reports the letter-channel abstention decode as **NOT RUN** with this
reason rather than as `0 of 0 passed`.

### 6.3 The FT letter channel paid a CPT-sized projection — FIXED

`fused_linear_cross_entropy` never *materialises* `[B, L, V]` logits, which is its stated
claim and is true. It also never *skipped* computing them where the mask said nothing was
supervised. Under CPT that is nearly free: `cpt_supervision` masks in almost every position.
Under FT it is the whole cost: `ft_supervision` masks in exactly **one** position per
non-span row.

Measured on real batch shapes at `V=13,787`, before the fix:

| batch | supervised | chunks | live | time, as the trainer calls it | same value, supervised only |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 42 x 479 | 42 of 20,076 | 9 | 9 | 0.381 s | 0.004 s (97x) |
| 11 x 2,947 | 11 of 32,406 | 14 | 11 | 0.514 s | 0.004 s (146x) |
| 6 x 5,383 | 6 of 32,292 | 14 | 6 | 0.477 s | 0.002 s (225x) |
| **1 x 34,522** | **1 of 34,521** | **15** | **1** | **0.511 s** | **0.005 s (112x)** |

Loss identical to 0.0 and `grad_hidden` to 1.9e-07 in every row of that table. Over one real
epoch the letter channel projected **1,459 position chunks of which 243 held a supervised
position** — recorded on every verdict row as `letter_channel_projection_waste`.

The fix skips a chunk whose mask is entirely false. Exactly, not approximately: the loss term
is `where(mask, …, 0).sum()` over an all-false mask, and `probs` is multiplied by that mask
before either gradient matmul, so `grad_hidden` lands on the zeros `zeros_like` already
wrote. Post-fix matmul counts, measured rather than timed (3 per projected chunk):
9→9, 14→11, 14→6, **15→1** chunks projected.

Pre-fix failure of the new test, against HEAD's `fused_ce.py` in a mirror:

```
AssertionError: 12 matmuls for 1 chunk(s) holding a supervised position; 4 chunks cover the
sequence, and projecting the 3 that hold none computes a slab that is then multiplied by zero
assert 12 <= ((3 * 1) + 2)
```

`test_the_matmul_instrument_sees_a_matmul_it_is_shown` passed in the same run, so the
instrument is asserted before the number it reports. One behaviour changes: with a non-finite
`lm_head`, `0 * inf` was `NaN` and could poison `grad_hidden` from a masked chunk; the
module's own contract is that a masked position contributes nothing to either gradient, so
contributing nothing is the stated behaviour and `NaN` was not.
`GAP-REALFT-FUSED-CE-PROJECTS-FULLY-MASKED-CHUNKS`.

**This is the finding with the largest blast radius on a rented GPU.** At 8K sequences and an
~80K vocabulary the projection is the dominant term of an FT step, and all but one chunk per
row of it was being thrown away.

### 6.4 The floor formulas are per batch, and a run is not — FIXED in this lane's tool

`ft_toy_run._letter_floor` and `_span_floor` group by prefix **inside one batch**. That is
the right number for what one batch's loss is reduced over, and the wrong bound on what a
*run* can reach: one set of parameters answers every batch, so two rows with the same prefix
and different golds are unfittable whether or not the sampler put them together — and a
per-batch computation cannot see the pair once it is split.

Measured on the real span rows: the mean per-batch floor is **0.223697** and the floor over
the plan is **0.693147**, a factor of 3.1. A run compared against the per-batch mean would
have looked like it was failing to converge by 3x while sitting exactly on its true floor.

Nothing is wrong in `tools/ft_toy_run.py`: its corpus is one batch repeated, so its per-batch
floor *is* its plan floor, and its calibration against a deliberately colliding corpus stands.
The generalisation only bites once a plan has more than one distinct batch, which is the
first thing a real shard set produces. `tools/real_ft_run.py._plan_floors` groups over the
plan and imports `_entropy` from `ft_toy_run` rather than restating it, so there is one owner
of the arithmetic. `GAP-REALFT-PER-BATCH-FLOOR-IS-NOT-A-BOUND-ON-A-RUN`.

### 6.5 This tool reproduced the FT lane's own verdict-row defect, and then fixed it

`GAP-FT-TOY-VERDICT-ROW-CONTRADICTED-ITS-OWN-RUN` is the record of a metric whose ledger row
said `passed=False` on a run the tool exited 0 on, because the exit status and the row were
computed from two different conditions. The first authoritative runs of `tools/real_ft_run.py`
did it again: `abstain_is_last_or_the_runtime_answers_a_line` bundled *"the span abstention is
unchanged between the two row orders"* (a control, which held) with *"every abstaining row
decodes as abstain"* (which this corpus forbids), so the row said `passed=False` while the
tool exited 0.

Fixed the same way the FT lane fixed theirs — by removing the second condition rather than
patching one of the two. `_counterfactual_holds` is now the single owner and both `main()`
and `_record_verdict` call it; the letter half is reported separately as `NotRun` with its
reason, because on this corpus there is nothing to flip and nothing-to-flip is not a pass.
The superseded rows are named in section 3.

### 6.6 A channel with no rows in the plan "reached its floor" — FIXED

**The one I nearly missed, found by asking the brief's question of my own tool.** 6.5 fixed a
metric whose ledger row disagreed with its exit status. It did not ask whether the *other*
channel had the same shape. It did.

`--max-width 479` keeps only bucket-0 batches. No letter row in this corpus is shorter than
1,359 tokens, so that plan is **1 batch, 42 sequences, slot kinds `['span']` of
`['choice', 'score', 'span']`** — zero letter rows. `_evaluate` returns `letter_gap = nan`,
and the exit-status check was:

```python

```

`nan` compares `False` against every bar, so **nothing was appended to `failures` for the
letter channel** — while `_record_verdict` wrote, for the same run:

            slot kinds ['span'] of ['choice', 'score', 'span']

The row says `passed=False`. The failure list says nothing about it. That is
`GAP-FT-TOY-VERDICT-ROW-CONTRADICTED-ITS-OWN-RUN` a second time, in the same tool, on the

floor, this run would have exited **0** with that `passed=False` letter row in the ledger.

**And it put four bare `NaN` tokens into the append-only ledger** — `NaN` is not valid JSON

The asymmetry was visible in the source and I had written it: the span metric was guarded

`corpus.letter_floor`, `corpus.span_floor` and both of `_plan_floors`' fallbacks went the
same way. `span_floor` had been substituting **`0.0`** for an empty plan — a vacuous zero,

zero rather than as nothing.

## 7. What did not run, and why

### 7.1 `mps` cannot train the widest real bucket — `GAP-REALFT-MPS-CANNOT-TRAIN-THE-WIDEST-REAL-BUCKET`

The real shard set's top bucket is 34,522 positions wide. `mps` fails on it in two different
ways depending on memory state, neither recoverable:

```
mps bucket 7 (1 x 34522): REFUSED exit 1: RuntimeError: MPSGaph does not support tensor
                                          dims larger than INT_MAX
```

and, in an isolated stage-by-stage probe, a **SIGSEGV** — the interpreter, not an exception:

```
Fatal Python error: Segmentation fault
  ... at::native::mps::tiled_bmm_out_mps_impl ...
  ... -[MPSNDArrayMultiaryKernel encodeToCommandEncoder:...] ...
```

Under `no_grad` the fused attention kernel succeeds in 2.4 s; **with** gradients torch falls
back to the math path, which materialises the attention matrix — `1 x 4 heads x 34,522²` is
4.77e9 against an `INT_MAX` of 2.15e9 — and the MPS tiled `bmm` dies. Buckets 0 through 6
(479 to 14,759) all run on `mps`. `cpu` takes the same shape in **3.128 s** — and in this
run's probe was faster than `mps` at every width, not only the one `mps` refuses; the full
table, and what those timings are and are not worth, is in section 3.

A `SIGSEGV` is not an exception: an in-process probe would take the tool, its ledger row and
every number it had measured with it. `tools/real_ft_run.py` therefore probes **each bucket
width in a subprocess** and treats the exit status as data, which is why arm 1 on `mps` is
reported NOT RUN with this reason rather than omitted or crashed into.

cap_hours` — priced from the **cap**, not from the hoped duration, so hitting the cap is not
