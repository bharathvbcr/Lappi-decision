## 1. The ruff version question, settled

**The gate is ruff 0.16.8, installed in `.venv`.** Not the standalone `ruff 0.15.20` at
`~/.local/bin/ruff`.

Reason: `ruff>=0.6` is declared in `[project.optional-dependencies].dev`, and **`uv.lock`
already resolves that constraint to 0.16.8** (`uv.lock:1190`). The lockfile is the
project's own answer to "which ruff"; a gate whose version depends on PATH order is a gate
whose findings are not reproducible. Installed with
`uv pip install --python .venv/bin/python 'ruff==0.16.8'` — not a new dependency, the
declared one. `pytest-cov>=5.0` was declared and missing too, and was installed with it.

Both versions were measured against the pre-fix tree and agreed exactly (87 findings in
`python/`, identical distribution), so the version choice did not change the work — but it
is now pinned, and `test_the_installed_ruff_is_the_version_the_lockfile_resolved` keeps it
pinned.

## 2. Two claims in the brief that did not survive measurement

**There are no E402 findings, and there never were in this measurement.** The brief listed
"15 E402". Direct measurement: `ruff check --select E402 python/` → *All checks passed*, on
both ruff versions. Ruff's E402 explicitly exempts imports that follow `sys.path` and
`os.environ` modification, which is exactly the shape every one of those test files has.
The brief's own arithmetic corroborates the misread: its per-rule list sums to 102 against
its stated total of 87, and 102 − 87 = 15. **The "15 E402" is the 15 `RUF100 Unused noqa
directive (unused: E402)` lines read as E402 findings.**

So the brief's instruction — decide whether each deliberate case is real and make the
deliberateness explicit rather than suppress it — was applied to the RUF100s instead, which
is where the question actually lived. The deliberate case *is* real (the import genuinely
must follow the `sys.path.insert`), and the `# noqa: E402` was never what made it explicit:
a noqa says "silence a rule", not "here is why". Ruff now exempts the pattern by rule, so
the directives were dead, and removing them is the fix — 15 of them, across 7 files.

The 12 `# noqa: E402` directives in the `pytest.importorskip` test files are a different
case: nothing exempts *those* imports, the directives are live, and they stayed. Section 4
is what happens when that distinction is not respected.

**`just` IS installed** at `/opt/homebrew/bin/just`, contrary to the brief. A Makefile was
still the right call and is what shipped — but the reason is "make is already here and adds
nothing new", not "just is unavailable".

## 4. The mistake this lane made, and the guard that now prevents it

Running `ruff check --select F401,RUF100 --fix` looked like a safe way to stage the
mechanical fixes. It is not. **RUF100 is evaluated against the *enabled* rule set**, so
narrowing `--select` to omit E402 makes ruff declare every `# noqa: E402` unused —
including the 12 live ones in the `pytest.importorskip` files, where nothing exempts the
imports. The `--fix` stripped them and took the tree from 0 E402 findings to 27.

Caught immediately by re-running E402 explicitly, reverted with `git checkout -- python/`
(after confirming no other lane had work in `python/`), and redone under the full
configured rule set, after which E402 stayed at 0.

Two guards now exist so it cannot recur silently:
`test_narrowing_the_rule_set_makes_ruff100_report_live_directives_as_dead` pins the
behaviour on a fixture, and `test_the_makefile_lint_target_does_not_narrow_the_rule_set`
asserts the gate passes no `--select`.
